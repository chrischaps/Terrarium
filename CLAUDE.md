# Terrarium

A LÖVE 11.5 / Lua game. Soft-bodied creatures (verlet worm, pressure blob, FABRIK spider) live in a jar, and each is built from a genome. Children inherit a blend of their parents plus mutation, and moss grazing does the selecting. The backlog is in GitHub issues and milestones on `chrischaps/Terrarium`.

## Running
- `love .`: the game.
- `lovec . --selftest`: Character's self-test, run against the jar (nav graph integrity, reachability, about 116 trips, determinism at 30/60/240 fps, a 10-minute replay of the whole jar, audio smoke test). Exits 1 on failure.
- `lovec . --replay <sec>`: open the jar twice from its seed, step both, and compare state hashes (`core/statehash.lua`). Prints `hash <hex>`, so separate runs can be compared too. A mismatch names the first part that drifted.
- `lovec . --test <name>[,<name>]`: just those self-tests (`graphs`, `trips`, `framerate`, `lantern`, `day`, `replay`, `genome`, `colour`, `worm`, `blob`, `spider`, `hatch`, `crowd`, `ranges`, `moss`, `temperament`, `court`, `eggs`, `death`, `food`, `audio`). A test is a `test(name, fn)` in `dev.lua`; new ones go there. Exits 2 on an unknown name. `--seed <n>` reseeds `day`, the M1 gate: all six residents wander, graze, rest through their own night (a night owl sleeps twice, from noon and again at dawn) and wake again over a 900-second day.
- Test modes (`--test`, `--selftest`, `--replay`, `--navdump`, `--headless`) run with no window: `conf.lua` turns it off and `core/headless.lua` stands in a null `love.graphics`, so composing the jar still bakes and draws from `rng.scape` the same way. `--window` opens one anyway. A headless jar must hash the same as a windowed one.
- `LOVE=lovec sh tests/ci.sh`: everything CI runs (`.github/workflows/selftest.yml`, Linux and Windows): the lint, the self-test, the replay hash compared across two processes, and with `WINDOW="xvfb-run -a"` a windowed jar against a headless one.
- `sh tests/check_random.sh`: fails on any `math.random`, `love.math.random` or stray `newRandomGenerator` outside `core/rng.lua`.
- `lovec . --snap - <sec> <file.png>`: fast-forward the sim, then save a screenshot to `%APPDATA%/LOVE/terrarium/`. Add `--nav` for every nav graph, or `--nav worm|blob|spider` for one with its jump arcs.
- `lovec . --script <file.lua>`: Character's frame-exact capture mode. The script returns `{run = function(api)}` and runs as a coroutine. Time moves only when it calls `api.step(sec)`, and `api.render(w, h, opts)` draws offscreen at any size. `api.open(seed, hour, level, day)` opens a jar, in its own room if it brings one, and `day` compresses its day as `--day` does. `opts.view = {x0, y0, x1, y1}` renders a plate: that stretch of the creatures' plane at full resolution, in front of the jar's frame enlarged and blurred. `scripts/lineup.lua` is the M2 gate (#19): 8 random genomes per plan on three shelves, by day and by night, saved as `lineup_day.png` and `lineup_night.png` (copy them to `renders/`, which git ignores).
- `lovec . --script scripts/pinch.lua`: a pinch of food dropped beside a blob under the lamp, saved as `pinch_1.png` (falling), `pinch_2.png` (down in the moss) and `pinch_3.png` (eaten).
- `lovec . --navdump <who> <solid>`: print a creature's nodes and edges on one solid.
- `--genome "worm segments=24 stiffness=0.9"` (or `"blob size=1.4 pressure=0.5"`, also read by `--navdump`) gives the first resident of that plan this genome (repeat it for the second). Otherwise each resident draws its genome from `rng.sim` at load (`world.genomes`).
- `--hour <h|name>` opens the jar at an hour (snaps default to midday), and `--day <sec>` compresses the day: `love . --day 120 --hour lampon`.
- `crowd` (#18) is the full jar: 16 of the heaviest bodies (24-segment worms, size 1.4 blobs, 8-legged spiders) on full soft bodies, and two steps of their sim must stay under 1/240 s, leaving most of a 60 fps frame to draw them. It draws between steps the way `main.lua` does, because eggs hatch between a frame's snapshot and its draw. For the drawn frame use `lovec . --crowd 16 --bench 20` (with `--hour 23` for night glow). `--crowd <n>` lays random eggs until the jar holds n, and `--bench <sec>` turns vsync off and prints the median, p95 and worst frame, update and draw ms once they've hatched. At 16, frames come in at about 5 ms, so there's no LOD. If a later change pushes the drawn frame over budget, the fallback is anchors plus a cheaper skin for distant creatures, and the simulation must never depend on it.
- `ranges` (#17) is the 200-genome run: 200 random genomes per plan, each alone in the jar, tour a mossy top, the shelf and home. It fails a genome that stalls, leaves the jar, sinks over 4px into the scenery or comes apart (`LIMITS` in `dev.lua`: a worm torn or knotted for 2 s, a blob crushed or inside out, a spider on feet it can't reach), and reports which quarter of each body gene's range the failures fall in. A failure means mend the body or shrink the range in `genome.lua`. The 30-day population run (M3) will register the same way and must stay runnable in CI.

## Module map (from the proposal)
- `genome.lua`: genes, ranges, crossover, mutation. One table per plan in the shape of Fishbowl's `species.lua`, genes in a fixed list (`real`, `int` or `pick`). Reals are kept to thousandths so `serialise`/`parse` round-trip exactly. A genome is `{plan = 'worm', segments = 14, ...}`. `Genome.child(a, b, gen)` breeds one, and `Genome.young(genome, grown)` is its body at `grown` (0 to 1): each body gene from the bottom of its range toward its own value, picks and temperament its own from the start
- `bodies/`: worm, blob and spider builders that take a genome (`Worm.new(x, y, world, genome)`: `segments` and `stiffness`; with no genome it's Character's 13 segments at the middle stiffness; `Blob.new` takes `size` and `pressure`, and `Blob.profile_for(size)` scales its radius, step and jump, so a blob's nav graph is its size's; `Spider.new` takes `legs` (half a side, in two alternating gait groups that step as one) and `reach`, and `Spider.profile_for(reach)` sets its crawl speed and `span`, the widest gap between solids its graph steps across) (`base.lua` holds the Mover anchor and Face; `fabrik.lua` is the spider's IK; `skin.lua` turns `hue`, `pattern` and `glow` into a body's colours: each plan owns a stretch of the OKLCH hue wheel and its own lightness, the four patterns are `capped`, `banded`, `spotted` and `striped`, and a body lists the markings it drew so `draw_emissive` can make them glow as the day cycle's `glow` rises. Skins and those lists are drawing, so `statehash` skips them)
- `nav/`: per-body navigation graph builder, A*, the ballistics jump model, and the path-following controller
- `core/`: `rng` (the `sim` and `look` streams), `statehash` (a fingerprint of the World for replay checks), `colour` (OKLCH to sRGB and back), `log`, `synth`
- `dev.lua`: argument parsing and the self-test
- `mind.lua`: needs and temperament choose goals (#21). Hunger (fed only by cropped moss) and its own clock pick grazing, wandering, following another creature, or resting (walkers on moss, spiders hanging under a ledge), or courting (#22). `Mind.meet` counts, once a step, each time two awake creatures of a plan come within `MEET` after time apart; a fed one that has met another `MET` times walks to `beside` it (the nodes nearest the two bodies' `court_gap`s apart) while the other waits, and the body reads `court` (partner, `beat` from the jar's clock, `side`) from `Mind:courting`: a worm's head loops a ring with its partner's half a turn away, a blob swells on the beat and leans in, a spider faces it and taps a front foot then the other. Markings flush on the beat (`Skin.shine`'s `flush`). Each courts once between sleeps, and the walker emits `court` and `courted` events. A hatchling is `young` until its last moult and doesn't court. The `court` test is #22's gate: in a 900 s day from lamp-on, every seed courts and every plan does. `boldness` sets how far from home it wanders and the biggest leap it takes: `Pathfinder.find(..., dare)` and `Controller:go_to(goal, dare)` leave out jumps and falls whose `edge.dare` (`NavGraph.dare`, the share of the body's spring a leap asks for) is above it, and hunger past `DESPERATE` dares anything. `sociability` weighs places by how near the others are and makes following likely, so the sociable sleep in piles. `chronotype` shifts its day against the lamp's along the gene's cube, so only the far end is a night owl. It only picks nodes from the creature's own graph, reads nothing of a body but its anchor, and keeps another creature as an index, so it can run on catch-up's coarse step (#31). The `temperament` test is #21's gate: opposite genes differ within a minute. It reads the hour from `World:hour()`, which comes from `opts.hour`/`opts.day`, both inputs like the seed. Treats (#25): crumbs down within `notice` (from boldness) of one that's up and `CURIOUS` (idle, wandering, following or grazing) bring it over (`to_treat`) to the place on the crumb's surface its `court_gap` away, on its own side and not where another is going, to `nibble` it (`TREAT` off its hunger). A crumb it can't reach goes in `passed`. The walker emits `nibbled`.
- `jar/`: tank, glass, day cycle and environment (from Fishbowl), plus the moss grid. `moss.lua` (#20) cuts every `moss = true` top into cells about 6 units wide, each a height from 0 (bare) to 1 (lush) under a ceiling set by how much of the lamp reaches it (the cone's pool, and three rays to the lid for shade). It grows only while the lamp is on (`DayCycle.lampAt(hour)`, pure, so the sim never touches the renderer's `env`), a grazing mind crops it (`Moss:graze` returns what was eaten, and that's what feeds hunger), and feet on a path wear it down to `Moss.WORN`, never bare. The heights are sim state in `world.moss` and in the hash. `Moss.draw` turns them into a cushion and tufts: thickness from height, colour from height over its ceiling, so shaded moss is short but green. The `moss` test wants half an hour of a morning to leave bare and worn cells with a quarter still lush, no growth at night, and every cropped cell back by the next afternoon. `food.lua` (#25) is the pinch of food, the player's one gift: `World:pinch(x)` (right-click in play) drops 5 to 8 crumbs from under the lid that turn over as they fall, glint in the lamp's pool, and settle on the first top below. The jar keeps `Food.PINCHES` (3) and one comes back every third of a day, so it stays a treat beside the moss. Uneaten crumbs are gone after `Food.KEEP` seconds. Crumbs are sim state (stepped before the minds, hashed with the World), a pinch is an input like the seed, and `food.given` keeps each one's `at` and `x` for the book. The `food` test is #25's gate: crumbs settle on tops, the awake ones within 200 come over and something is eaten, none comes from past its notice, the same pinch replays the same, and the pantry empties and refills on time. It also holds `world.lua` (creatures, graphs and controllers on the fixed step), `level.lua`/`collision.lua` (AABB solids), and `room.lua` (the jar's solids in Character's level format, 2000x780: soil, a one-footed cork arch, a standing stone and cairn with a twig between them over a water dish, a flat stone under the lamp, a cork root and log either side of a stone tor, and a slate shelf; `moss = true` tags the tops the moss grid will cover, and `residents` lists who lives there: two of each kind, each with a placeholder name and a spawn point). Each body's graph is built from its `nav` profile, not its own (`X.nav_profile(genome)`): sizes and reaches come in 8 buckets, and a bucket's graph is built for its most limited body (the biggest blob's clearance with the smallest one's spring and step, the shortest legs' span). `min_radius` makes every clearance and arc check cover all the radii in the bucket at once, because a small body flies lower and isn't simply safer. Every worm shares Character's graph. Graphs are cached per World in `world.navs` (`world.graphs[i]`; `world.graph_of[key]` is the first resident's). The residents' graphs are built whole at load. `World:lay(genome, x, solid, name, opts)` lays an egg, and an uncached graph is then built `World.BUILD_SLICE` units per step (`NavGraph.builder`). With no opts it hatches grown as soon as its graph is ready (`--crowd`, `hatch`). Eggs and growing up (#23): on `courted`, `World:clutch` lays 1 to 3 children (`Genome.child`) in a row on the mossy top nearest the pair, while creatures plus eggs stay under `World.CAPACITY` (16). Each is `opts.young` and `opts.due` about a jar day later (`World:day_length()`), and waits for the graphs of all `World.STAGES + 1` bodies it will grow through, so they're built while it incubates. It hatches as `Genome.young(genome, 0)` with a pale skin (`Skin.of(key, genome, young)`), and `World:moult` grows it a stage in place (`Worm:grow` adds tail segments, `Blob:grow` swells about its feet, `Spider:grow` lengthens its legs and replants), once per sleep and no sooner than `World.GROW` days over the stages, swapping in its new graph and controller. `world.young[i]` holds its stage until it's grown. The walker emits `laid`, `hatched` and `moulted`. `jar/eggs.lua` draws the clutches (a worm's lemon-shaped cocoon, a blob's frogspawn bead, a spider's silk sac, in the child's pale colours, glowing as `glow` and the night allow, trembling near hatching) and `world.shells` for `World.SHELL` seconds. The `eggs` test is #23's gate: a clutch from a courted pair hatches a day later, small and pale, and grows into its own genome in three moults, a child of both parents. Ageing and death (#24): `world.lives[i]` holds each creature's `born` and `span` (`World.LIFE`, 3 to 5 jar days from `rng.sim`; a resident has already lived up to `World.LIVED` of them). Past `World.ELDER` of its life it's an elder: `World:age` slows its controller (`Controller.pace`) and deepens its skin (`Skin.age`, `old`). When its time comes its mind is `last` (no courting, and its last bed is on moss, a spider's too), and it dies in its sleep (`dying`, after `World.GRACE` days awake it dies wherever it settles), its eyes close (`Face.closing`) and it fades for `World.FADE` seconds. Then `World:return_to_moss` presses the moss it lay on flat and makes it rich (`Moss:enrich`: a higher ceiling, faster and greener growth, used up over about three days of lamp), and `World:remove` takes it out and renumbers every index (minds' `met`/`near`/`with`, `young`, `selected`, the render snapshots). Anything holding a creature index outside the World must listen for `returned` (the lantern does) or hold the creature and use `World:index_of`. `jar/remains.lua` draws a dying body through a canvas, sinking, going moss-coloured (`Skin.age`'s `earth`) and fading, with spores rising from it and from `world.remains` for `World.SPORES` seconds. The walker emits `dying` and `returned`. The `death` test is #24's gate: an elder of each plan slows and deepens, dies asleep on the moss, fades in `FADE`, leaves the jar's indices whole and its moss regrown taller by the next afternoon, and two runs agree after a death. The work is counted, never timed, so the egg hatches on the same step on every machine. The `hatch` test lays the worst clutch of 16 and checks that no frame reaches 1/60 s. `World:first(key)` finds the first one of a kind. `ballistics.solve_jump(..., level)` returns the lowest arc the body flies clear of the level, and the graph and the controller both ask it that way. `scenery.lua` bakes and draws those solids from `rng.scape`. Seen side-on, a roof grounded at both ends seals the floor under it, so `NavGraph:keep_home` keeps only each body's largest mutually reachable set of nodes. The view comes from Fishbowl: `config.lua` (projection, box, creature plane `PLANE_D`, and `S`, the world px per level unit that keeps the residents small in a wide tank; convert with `Tank.wx`/`wy`/`lx`/`ly`, never `C.OX + x`), `tank.lua` (project/unproject, and `Tank.pushPlane()` for drawing in level coordinates), `render.lua` (canvases, depth-of-field blur), `scape.lua` (room, desk, soil bed and layers, stones) and `glass.lua` (panes, lid, front glass, the lamp's cone). Light comes from `daycycle.lua`: a jar day is a real day, and the hour is `DayCycle.hourAt(startHour, world.time)`, so it's sim time, never the wall clock (that's read once at load, like the seed). `plants.lua` (ferns, moss sporophytes; it's World's `flora`, stepped on the fixed step) and `motes.lua`. `condensation.lua` fogs the inside of the front glass from `env.damp` (a day-cycle key) and the hour. It's a pure function of the jar's clock, composed from `rng.scape` last, and drawn with `shaders/mist.glsl`. `lantern.lua` is the watch-only follow light. It lives in `main.lua`, outside the World, and reads only `interp_points()`. Never route it through `world.selected`, because selection changes who wanders. Shaders live in `shaders/` (`floor.glsl` relights the soil bed).
- `core/rng.lua` has three streams: `sim`, `look`, and `scape`. `scape` is the jar's fixed composition. It's seeded from the jar seed and drawn from only at load
- Files copied from Character carry a `-- From chrischaps/Character @ <sha> (<path>)` header. Their internal requires were remapped (`creatures.` → `bodies.`, `core.navgraph` → `nav.navgraph`, `world.` → `jar.`). Other edits are marked `-- Terrarium:`.
- `book.lua`: save data and the family book
- Require path is `lib/?.lua`, so LoveLibs modules load as `require('LoveLibs.<area>.<module>')`, the same as in [Character](https://github.com/chrischaps/Character), where it's `libs/`. Code ported from Character keeps its requires unchanged.
- `lib/LoveLibs/`: git subtree of chrischaps/LoveLibs. Don't edit it in place. Update with `git subtree pull --prefix=lib/LoveLibs https://github.com/chrischaps/LoveLibs.git master --squash`

## Rules
- **Deterministic.** Simulation advances only on the fixed 120 Hz step. All randomness comes from the jar's seeded RNG; never call `math.random` or `love.math.random` directly. Rendering must never affect the simulation.
- **The body never lies.** A creature is only offered paths its own body can travel, so rebuild its nav graph from its body.
- **Every trait is visible.** A gene that changes nothing you can see within a minute is cut.
- **You witness; you don't breed.** The player's verbs are watching, following with the lantern, naming, and dropping in food.
- **Gentle mortality.** Death is shown as returning to the moss.
