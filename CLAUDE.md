# Terrarium

A LÖVE 11.5 / Lua game. Soft-bodied creatures (verlet worm, pressure blob, FABRIK spider) live in a jar, and each is built from a genome. Children inherit a blend of their parents plus mutation, and moss grazing does the selecting. The backlog is in GitHub issues and milestones on `chrischaps/Terrarium`.

## Running
- `love .`: the game.
- `lovec . --selftest`: Character's self-test, run against the jar (nav graph integrity, reachability, about 116 trips, determinism at 30/60/240 fps, a 10-minute replay of the whole jar, and audio: voices from genes, courtship on its beat, a heard jar the same as an unheard one). Exits 1 on failure.
- `lovec . --replay <sec>`: open the jar twice from its seed, step both, and compare state hashes (`core/statehash.lua`). Prints `hash <hex>`, so separate runs can be compared too. A mismatch names the first part that drifted. With `--window` both runs are also drawn as they go (every 30 steps, through the style), so it checks that watching changes nothing.
- `lovec . --test <name>[,<name>]`: just those self-tests (`graphs`, `trips`, `framerate`, `lantern`, `day`, `replay`, `genome`, `colour`, `worm`, `blob`, `spider`, `springtail`, `snail`, `hatch`, `crowd`, `ranges`, `moss`, `temperament`, `court`, `eggs`, `death`, `floor`, `month`, `catchup`, `food`, `book`, `audio`, `cast`, `frame`, `styles`); `--skip <name>[,<name>]` runs all but those. A test is a `test(name, fn)` in `dev.lua`; new ones go there. Exits 2 on an unknown name. `--seed <n>` reseeds `day`, the M1 gate, and brings that seed's cast: all six residents wander, graze, rest through their own night (a night owl sleeps twice, from noon and again at dawn) and wake again over a 900-second day.
- Test modes (`--test`, `--selftest`, `--replay`, `--navdump`, `--headless`) run with no window: `conf.lua` turns it off and `core/headless.lua` stands in a null `love.graphics`, so composing the jar still bakes and draws from `rng.scape` the same way. `--window` opens one anyway. A headless jar must hash the same as a windowed one.
- `LOVE=lovec sh tests/ci.sh`: everything CI runs on each push and PR (`.github/workflows/selftest.yml`, Linux and Windows): the lint, the self-test but for the long gates (`--skip month,eggs,floor,ranges`, #112: together about half an hour of each run), the replay hash compared across two processes and once more interpreted (`--interpret`, the compiler off), and with `WINDOW="xvfb-run -a"` a jar watched in a window, in every style (`--styles` lists them), against a headless one. `GAME=dist/terrarium.love` runs it all against a packed game instead of the checkout. `LONG=1` runs the long gates too: `eggs`, `floor` and `ranges` in the self-test, and the month's ten seeds as separate processes side by side (`JOBS` at a time). `release.yml` sets it, so every release passes them all; by hand it's about an hour. Each one also runs alone as `--test <name>`, and the self-test prints how long each test took.
- `sh scripts/package.sh [ref]` (#91): pack a commit (HEAD by default) as `dist/terrarium.love` with `dist/SHA256SUMS` beside it. It's a `git archive`, so only what's committed ships, without `tests/`, CI, the claim tools or `CLAUDE.md`. Pushing a `v*` tag runs `.github/workflows/release.yml`: it packs the tag, runs `tests/ci.sh` against that `.love` on Linux and Windows, and drafts a release with both files (a tag with a hyphen, like `v0.1.0-frame.1`, drafts a prerelease). Publishing the draft by hand is the gate; Easel installs from published releases. Run the workflow by hand for a trial that drafts nothing; a PR that touches `package.sh`, `ci.sh` or the workflow gets one too.
- `sh tests/check_random.sh`: fails on any `math.random`, `love.math.random` or stray `newRandomGenerator` outside `core/rng.lua`.
- `love . --frame` (#90): the Pi frame, a Raspberry Pi 5 behind a 7" Touch Display 2 resting in landscape at 1280 × 720, which its launcher Easel starts with `love terrarium.love --frame` and stops with SIGTERM. Borderless fullscreen at the display's size, no cursor, no keys. In a window, `core/screen.lua`'s `Screen.fit` fits the 1280 × 800 view to any screen by covering it, losing at most `Screen.CROP` (10%) of the view's height or width, and bars beyond that (1280 × 720 loses 40 px from the top and bottom and keeps the room round the jar). On the frame (#110) `Screen.tank` makes the jar the whole picture: the glass's inside (`Glass.innerRect()`, taken in by `Screen.INSET`, 3 px, past the glass's colour fringes) covers the screen, so no wall, desk or jar frame shows. At 1280 × 720 it's drawn at about 1.17, lid to base, and the sides give way, about 40 px of screen each; the lamp's shade (above the lid) goes, and its light through the lid stays. The open book is still drawn through `Screen.fit`, so none of it is lost. The closed book's desk is off the screen, so on the frame it's found by its red ribbon, up over the bottom edge `Pages.TAB_X` from the right (`Pages:drawTab`, with a slip of paper beside it when there's news); a tap there opens it. `api.render(w, h, {frame = true})` renders a frame as the frame shows it, and `{fit = f}` through any fit. `core/touch.lua` reads a finger: a tap follows as a click does, and holding still for `Touch.HOLD` (0.6 s) drops a pinch, with a ring of lamplight closing round the finger as it holds (opening out as the pinch falls, shrinking away if the jar has none left). In frame mode the left mouse button reads the same, in case the screen arrives as a pointer. Every way out goes through `love.quit` (SDL turns SIGTERM into a quit), which, if `$EASEL_DATA` is set, saves the last frame to `$EASEL_DATA/thumb.png` at 640 × 360 for the launcher's card, framed as the frame is. The book (#30) saves there too once it lands. Load prints `[frame] <w>x<h> ...` and flushes it, so a launcher knows the jar is up. The `frame` test checks both fits (the glass meets every edge of the frame, the card is the same picture, a tap comes back to the point on the plane, the ribbon opens the book and the glass doesn't) and the gestures, and `tests/ci.sh` (under `$WINDOW`) starts `--frame`, sends SIGTERM and wants a clean exit and a thumb.
- `lovec . --snap - <sec> <file.png>`: fast-forward the sim, then save a screenshot to `%APPDATA%/LOVE/terrarium/`. Add `--nav` for every nav graph, or `--nav worm|blob|spider` for one with its jump arcs. `--style <name>` draws it in a style.
- `lovec . --script <file.lua>`: Character's frame-exact capture mode. The script returns `{run = function(api)}` and runs as a coroutine. Time moves only when it calls `api.step(sec)`, and `api.render(w, h, opts)` draws offscreen at any size. `api.open(seed, hour, level, day)` opens a jar, in its own room if it brings one, and `day` compresses its day as `--day` does. `opts.view = {x0, y0, x1, y1}` renders a plate: that stretch of the creatures' plane at full resolution, in front of the jar's frame enlarged and blurred. `scripts/lineup.lua` is the M2 gate (#19): 8 random genomes per plan on three shelves, by day and by night, saved as `lineup_day.png` and `lineup_night.png` (copy them to `renders/`, which git ignores).
- `lovec . --script scripts/pinch.lua`: a pinch of food dropped beside a blob under the lamp, saved as `pinch_1.png` (falling), `pinch_2.png` (down in the moss) and `pinch_3.png` (eaten).
- `lovec . --script scripts/book.lua` (#32): a week in one jar of 900 s days, everyone sitting for portraits as in play, the book shut at dawn on the last day but one, then printed as text and saved spread by spread as `book_01.png` (the title, and while you were away) on, with `book_desk.png` (closed on the desk, a slip of paper in it) and `book_turn.png` (a leaf turning). `BOOK_DAYS=2` for fewer days. About four minutes.
- `lovec . --script scripts/timelapse.lua [--seed <n>] [--day <sec>] [--cast a,b,c]` (#33): a month in one jar. The classic cast on 900 s days, opened at dawn, watched whole from where you'd stand for 30 jar days. It eases from fifteen times life up to 2 s of film a jar day and down again, so it ends as it began, with the lamp coming on (about 72 s of film). Each frame is `SUB` (4) renders spread over the jar time it stands for, a long shutter. A tally low on the left gains a stroke each dawn. Frames go raw into `ffmpeg` (on the PATH), cropped to 16:9 as the frame crops and scaled to 1920 × 1080. It leaves `timelapse_<seed>.mp4` and `timelapse_<seed>.txt` (the chronicle: every clutch, hatching, moult and return, with each one's generation) in the save directory. It yields after every frame: a script that renders without yielding never lets LÖVE present, and the driver queues every frame until memory runs out. With `--headless` it writes only the chronicle, in about ten minutes. Takes about a quarter of an hour.
- `lovec . --script scripts/moments.lua [--seed <n>]` (#33): the same jar's first courtships, clutches, hatchings, moults and returns to the moss over 8 days, each a plate framed on where it happened, as `moment_<seed>_<n>_<kind>.png`.
- `love . --style <name>` (or Tab in play, or `api.style(name)` in a script) draws the finished frame in a style (#94, epic #93). A style is `{name, shader, setup(sh, w, h, time)}` in `jar/styles.lua`, its shader in `shaders/styles/` loaded through `Render.shader` (lazily; one that fails to compile prints its error and the frame goes through unstyled). `main.lua`'s `composite` runs it on the finished frame (the scene, or a script's plate) before the glass refraction, vignette and grain, so the window, `--snap` and `api.render` all show the same look. `none` is the old frame byte for byte and the default; `silver`, a toned grey print, is the seam's test. `pencil` (#95) is a sketchbook page: Sobel edges on the log of the light (its floor a little of the light around, so a busy texture in the lamp's pool draws no more lines than a stone's edge in the dark) as a graphite line that boils `BOILS` (4) times a second and only then, the shade as hatching laid on the page (a first layer, a steeper one crossing it, a flatter third for the darkest, with graphite rubbed in under them), and the paper (`Styles.PAPER`, made by hashing, no stream) its tooth and mottle. The hatching never reads the clock, so it holds still while the jar does. `pencil-each` is its A/B: a style with `each` has main.lua's `drawCreatures` draw every creature alone onto a clear canvas (`Styles.body`, scissored to its bounds) and lay it in through `pencil_body.glsl`, its outline from its own silhouette and its hatching anchored to its middle so the strokes travel with it, taking it out of the frame's alpha so the whole-frame pass (`masked`) leaves it be; anything in front of the plane puts the alpha back. Both share `pencil_common.glsl` (`Render.shader` resolves `#include "file"` within a shader's folder). `lovec . --script scripts/pencil_ab.lua` films the busiest creature in both, side by side, as `ab_<day|evening>_<nnn>.png`. A style may set `glass = {aberration, grain}` for what the composite lays over it (`Styles.GLASS` by default; paper takes neither). A style sees pixels only. `lovec . --script scripts/styles.lua` saves the jar in every style, day and night, plus a plate, as `style_<name>_<day|night|plate>.png`.
- `lovec . --headless --script scripts/listen.lua`: #28's listening test. A jar from just before lamp-on on 900 s days, heard onto a tape (`core/tape.lua`) until it has done everything once (the first to lay is aged so it goes back to the moss that night), then those scenes mixed into `listen.wav` in the save directory with a cue sheet: lamp on, a pinch, a courtship and its clutch, a hatching, a moult, lamp off, a last sigh, the return to the moss. Takes a few minutes.
- `love . --away <time>` (#31): open a jar left that long ago (`2d`, `36h`, `90m` in jar days, hours and minutes, so `--day` shortens them, or plain seconds) and catch it up. It opens at the hour less the time away, so it comes back at the hour it should. With `--snap`, `--script` or `--bench` it's caught up at once; in play it's the moment (`jar/passing.lua`). The book (#30) will start the same thing from a saved jar and the time since it was saved: `Passing.begin(world, away)`.
- `lovec . --script scripts/away.lua`: a jar left in the evening and opened three and a half days later, saved as `away_000.png` and on, every `AWAY_EVERY` (12) frames of the moment at 30 fps (`AWAY_EVERY=1` for a film of it).
- `lovec . --navdump <who> <solid>`: print a creature's nodes and edges on one solid.
- `--genome "worm segments=24 stiffness=0.9"` (or `"blob size=1.4 pressure=0.5"`, also read by `--navdump`) gives the first resident of that plan this genome (repeat it for the second). Otherwise each resident draws its genome from `rng.sim` at load (`world.genomes`).
- `--hour <h|name>` opens the jar at an hour (snaps default to midday), and `--day <sec>` compresses the day: `love . --day 120 --hour lampon`.
- `crowd` (#18) is the full jar: 16 of the heaviest bodies (24-segment worms, size 1.4 blobs, 8-legged spiders) on full soft bodies, and two steps of their sim must stay under 1/240 s, leaving most of a 60 fps frame to draw them. It draws between steps the way `main.lua` does, because eggs hatch between a frame's snapshot and its draw. For the drawn frame use `lovec . --crowd 16 --bench 20` (with `--hour 23` for night glow). `--crowd <n>` lays random eggs until the jar holds n, and `--bench <sec>` turns vsync off and prints the median, p95 and worst frame, update and draw ms once they've hatched. At 16, frames come in at about 5 ms, so there's no LOD. If a later change pushes the drawn frame over budget, the fallback is anchors plus a cheaper skin for distant creatures, and the simulation must never depend on it.
- `ranges` (#17) is the 200-genome run: 200 random genomes per plan, each alone in the jar, tour a mossy top, the shelf and home. It fails a genome that stalls, leaves the jar, sinks over 4px into the scenery or comes apart (`LIMITS` in `dev.lua`: a worm torn or knotted for 2 s, a blob crushed or inside out, a spider on feet it can't reach), and reports which quarter of each body gene's range the failures fall in. A failure means mend the body or shrink the range in `genome.lua`. The 30-day population run (M3) registers the same way.
- `month` (#27) is the M3 gate: ten jars (`MONTH_SEEDS`), each left alone from lamp-on for 30 jar days of real length on the coarse step. The jar never holds fewer than 4 or more than `CAPACITY` (eggs counted, the dying not), no kind is ever gone, every founder has gone back to the moss, and every kind reaches `MONTH_GENERATIONS` (4) generations, so the floor isn't what's holding it up. It logs each kind's count per day, each gene's mean and spread from the founders to the living, and writes every day's to `month_<seed>.csv` in the save directory (a release's CI run keeps them). A seed takes about 90 s; `--seed <n>` runs one, and `--day 900` makes a month a second. It also checks that two coarse runs agree. The coarse step: `World.new(..., {coarse = true})` makes each creature a `Token` (`bodies/token.lua`: its plan's `take` with no soft body, answering what minds and the World ask of a body) that a `Glide` (`nav/glide.lua`, Controller's interface) moves along its own graph's edges at its body's speed, a leap taking its edge's time. Step it `World.COARSE` (1) s at a time. Minds, moss, eggs, ageing and the floor are unchanged, so it's what catch-up (#31) runs on. It can't match a live jar step for step, only be deterministic and consistent.
- `catchup` (#31) is catch-up's gate: a live jar caught mid-stride lives a day away in under `AWAY_BUDGET` (8 s; about 2 s here) and comes back at the right hour, all bodies, each whole by its plan's `LIMITS` for `AWAY_AFTER` (20) s; a month away on a 900 s day lives `Catchup.CAP` (7) days less the whole days over, through hatchings and deaths, and comes back the same; two runs agree, however the steps were split between frames. Watched for longer, a young worm grazing sometimes stays knotted for several seconds, and a jar that never went away does that too.

## Module map (from the proposal)
- `genome.lua`: genes, ranges, crossover, mutation. One table per plan in the shape of Fishbowl's `species.lua`, genes in a fixed list (`real`, `int` or `pick`). Reals are kept to thousandths so `serialise`/`parse` round-trip exactly. A genome is `{plan = 'worm', segments = 14, ...}`. `Genome.child(a, b, gen)` breeds one, and `Genome.young(genome, grown)` is its body at `grown` (0 to 1): each body gene from the bottom of its range toward its own value, picks and temperament its own from the start
- `bodies/`: worm, blob, spider, springtail and snail builders that take a genome (`Worm.new(x, y, world, genome)`: `segments` and `stiffness`; with no genome it's Character's 13 segments at the middle stiffness; `Blob.new` takes `size` and `pressure`, and `Blob.profile_for(size)` scales its radius, step and jump, so a blob's nav graph is its size's; `Spider.new` takes `legs` (half a side, in two alternating gait groups that step as one) and `reach`, and `Spider.profile_for(reach)` sets its crawl speed and `span`, the widest gap between solids its graph steps across; `Springtail.new` (#76) takes `spring`, how hard its furcula throws it, so `Springtail.profile_for(spring)` sets its jump and `hop_max`, bucketed like reach, and `antennae`, their length and nothing else: a three-point verlet capsule on six two-bone legs that walks only to line up a leap, somersaults in the air (half a turn when thrown slower than `TUMBLE_VX`, a whole one faster, never by chance) and rolls the rest out upright as it lands, legs folded until it has settled; courting, a pair hop in place on `HOP_BEAT` with antennae touching; asleep (the World's `ctx.rest`) it sits low with legs tucked and antennae back; dying it curls; `Snail.new` (#77) takes `size` (its body and crawl speed, bucketed; drawn `k` = size × `BODY` big) and `whorls`, the turns of its shell's log spiral (`Snail.spiral`, a fan mesh per whorls; scaled one turn in, it's every whorl but the last, which draws `capped`): a verlet foot whose points ahead of the anchor walk the real surface (`Climb.along`) and behind it follow the track its sole has laid (`find_sole`, the anchor brought down onto the surface, so a corner's join is gone round, not cut), pedal waves running forward and the shell rocking on them, eye stalks that pull in when something comes near (a `withdraw` event); turning round, its foot draws in under its shell and out the other way as the shell turns edge-on (`turn`); a ribbon of `slime` from its sole that shines in the lamp's pool and dries in `DRY` s (drawing only, in statehash's `SKIP`); asleep it seals itself in on the glass or under a ledge (`Snail.rest`, which `mind.lua`'s beds ask a plan) behind a pale epiphragm; courting, a pair reach out until their heads meet, then rear up and sway on `SWAY_BEAT`, feelers touching; dying, its body fades but its shell (`draw_kept`) doesn't, and stays in the moss after (`Snail.keeps`, `World.KEPT`, `Snail.draw_left`) as the moss grows over it) (`base.lua` holds the Mover anchor and Face; `climb.lua` (#77) is a climber's controller interface, `Climb.into(Class)`, shared by the spider and snail; `fabrik.lua` is the spider's IK; `skin.lua` turns `hue`, `pattern` and `glow` into a body's colours: each plan owns a stretch of the OKLCH hue wheel and its own lightness (six families since #75, the snail, springtail and moth ahead of their bodies; the `colour` test compares every family in `Skin.families` at 0.18 apart in OKLab, as sRGB shows them, since `oklch` gives up chroma it can't show; a family's `larva` colours, read by `Skin.larva`, are the moth's inchworm, leaf-green with its parent's pink), the four patterns are `capped`, `banded`, `spotted` and `striped`, and a body lists the markings it drew so `draw_emissive` can make them glow as the day cycle's `glow` rises. Skins and those lists are drawing, so `statehash` skips them)
- `nav/`: per-body navigation graph builder, A*, the ballistics jump model, and the path-following controller. Ways of moving are flags on the profile, never a body's name: `climbs`, `leaps = false` (no jumps or falls, so no ballistic edges) and `flies` (kept for the moth, #78; nothing reads it yet), and for a hopper (#76) `hops` (jumps also join nodes of one span up to `hop_max` apart), `walk_cost` (walk edges cost that times their length), `jump_flat` (what a leap costs over 1.25 times its length, 60 by default) and `loft` (`ballistics.solve_jump` never arcs lower than that over the higher end). A climber's ring of nodes round a solid joins two neighbours only along open face (`NavGraph:open_face`, #77), so no crawl edge runs through a solid that covers part of another (the arch's lip; the twig lies flush on the stone and cairn, so the dish under it is shut off for climbers too). No edge costs less than its chord, so A* stays admissible. The controller repaths after `Controller:patience(i)`, which is 7 s plus that edge's length at the body's speed and pace, and `Controller:eta()` is the rest of its path's cost over that speed, which the `trips` and `ranges` budgets are built on.
- **A plan is one registration (#73).** A new kind is a body module in `World.CREATURES`, a gene plan in `Genome.order`, a family in `Skin.families` (with its `shell` colour), an egg in `jar/eggs.lua`'s `SIZE`/`DRAW`, a pair's homes in `room.lua`'s `homes` (and `grazes = true` in `World.CREATURES` if it feeds on the soil's moss), and, if it makes any sound, an entry in `Synth.SOUNDS` (with none it's silent). Nothing else names it. Its body module carries what the self-test asks of it: `LIMITS` (the measures its `health(c, m, dt)` folds into `m`, the worst so far, with how far each may go and how a fault reads), `grown_size(c)`, `heaviest(genome)`, `half(t)` (how far either side of its anchor a token of it lies, for where it goes back to the moss), optional `length(genome)` (how far it lies out behind its anchor, for the lineup) and, if its graphs are bucketed, `BUCKETS` and `BUCKET_GENE`. Every body carries a `Base.Mover` (`World:place` asserts it). Optional: `rest(node)` (where it sleeps, read by `mind.lua`'s beds), `APPETITE` (how fast its hunger rises against the others', 1 if absent; a snail's is 0.25, since a slow body that hungers as fast spends its day crawling to dinner and never courts), and `keeps(c)` with `draw_left(r, world, u, light_dir)` and a body's `draw_kept(ctx)` (a part of it that stays after it dies, #77). A home may give a climber `y` up a wall's face instead of `x` on a top (the snail on the glass); the first home of a kind must be on a top, since the self-test lays its eggs beside it. `dev.lua` loops over `Genome.order`, or over `kinds(w)` (the plans a jar holds), and skips a plan with no home in the room.
- `core/`: `rng` (the `sim`, `look`, `scape` and `sound` streams), `statehash` (a fingerprint of the World for replay checks), `colour` (OKLCH to sRGB and back), `log`, `synth`, `tape`, `fmath` (`sq(x)`, the sim's square: in LuaJIT `x ^ 2` is `pow` interpreted and `x * x` compiled, about one in 500 a bit apart, #94)
- `core/synth.lua` (#28): the jar heard through its glass. Every sound is baked at load through two poles of low-pass and two short combs (the jar's hollow), and plays at `QUIET`. Each plan's sounds, voice genes and courtship beat are its `Synth.SOUNDS` entry. `Synth.voice(c, old)` reads a body's genes as they are now: the size gene (`segments`, `size`, `reach`) sets pitch over about an octave, so a hatchling is high and deepens as it moults, an elder's drops a fifth of an octave, and the give gene (`stiffness`, `pressure`, `legs`) picks one of three variants of its sounds. It hears the World's events (`jump`, `land`, `step`, `courted`, `laid`, `hatched`, `moulted`, `dying`, `returned`, `nibbled`, `pinch`) and each `update` reads state: the lamp's timer clicks and hum (`DayCycle.lampAt`), crumbs landing, the movement loops, and courtship on each body's beat (`Worm.COIL_SPIN`, `Blob.PULSE`, `Spider.TAP_BEAT`, scaled by the body's `ardour`). The one the lantern follows is louder. It never writes to the World and draws only from `rng.sound`; the `audio` test checks a heard jar hashes the same as an unheard one. `main.lua` makes one only in live play (not `--snap` or `--script`). `m` mutes it, and `--mute` opens the jar muted. Given a `Tape`, it records instead of playing, and `Tape:bounce(scenes, file)` mixes stretches of it into a stereo WAV
- `dev.lua`: argument parsing and the self-test
- `mind.lua`: needs and temperament choose goals (#21). Hunger (fed only by cropped moss, and never past `STARVING`) and its own clock pick grazing, wandering, following another creature, or resting (walkers on moss, spiders hanging under a ledge), or courting (#22). `Mind.meet` counts, once a step, each time two awake creatures of a plan come within `MEET` after time apart; a fed one that has met another `MET` times walks to `beside` it (the nodes nearest the two bodies' `court_gap`s apart) while the other waits (for `REACH`, or half as long again as the way's `eta` for a slow body, #77; `Glide:eta` answers it on the coarse step), and the body reads `court` (partner, `beat` from the jar's clock, `side`) from `Mind:courting`: a worm's head loops a ring with its partner's half a turn away, a blob swells on the beat and leans in, a spider faces it and taps a front foot then the other. Markings flush on the beat (`Skin.shine`'s `flush`). Each courts once between sleeps, and the walker emits `court` and `courted` events. A hatchling is `young` until its last moult and doesn't court. The `court` test is #22's gate: in a 900 s day from lamp-on, every seed courts and every plan does. `boldness` sets how far from home it wanders and the biggest leap it takes: `Pathfinder.find(..., dare)` and `Controller:go_to(goal, dare)` leave out jumps and falls whose `edge.dare` (`NavGraph.dare`, the share of the body's spring a leap asks for) is above it, and hunger past `DESPERATE` dares anything. `sociability` weighs places by how near the others are and makes following likely, so the sociable sleep in piles. `chronotype` shifts its day against the lamp's along the gene's cube, so only the far end is a night owl. It only picks nodes from the creature's own graph, reads nothing of a body but its anchor, and keeps another creature as an index, so it can run on catch-up's coarse step (#31). The `temperament` test is #21's gate: opposite genes differ within a minute. It reads the hour from `World:hour()`, which comes from `opts.hour`/`opts.day`, both inputs like the seed. Treats (#25): crumbs down within `notice` (from boldness) of one that's up and `CURIOUS` (idle, wandering, following or grazing) bring it over (`to_treat`) to the place on the crumb's surface its `court_gap` away, on its own side and not where another is going, to `nibble` it (`TREAT` off its hunger). A crumb it can't reach goes in `passed`. The walker emits `nibbled`.
- `jar/`: tank, glass, day cycle and environment (from Fishbowl), plus the moss grid. `moss.lua` (#20) cuts every `moss = true` top into cells about 6 units wide, each a height from 0 (bare) to 1 (lush) under a ceiling set by how much of the lamp reaches it (the cone's pool, and three rays to the lid for shade). ## Rules
- **Deterministic.** Simulation advances only on the fixed 120 Hz step. All randomness comes from the jar's seeded RNG; never call `math.random` or `love.math.random` directly. Rendering must never affect the simulation: drawing keeps what it needs (screen positions, meshes) off sim tables or in statehash's `SKIP`. A square in sim code is `sq(x)` (`core/fmath.lua`) or `x * x`, never `x ^ 2`: LuaJIT works `^ 2` out one way interpreted and another compiled, so a jar would come out different depending on what each process had compiled when. `tests/ci.sh` replays the jar with `--interpret` (the compiler off) and wants the same hash.
- **The body never lies.** A creature is only offered paths its own body can travel, so rebuild its nav graph from its body.
- **Every trait is visible.** A gene that changes nothing you can see within a minute is cut.
- **You witness; you don't breed.** The player's verbs are watching, following with the lantern, naming, and dropping in food.
- **Gentle mortality.** Death is shown as returning to the moss.

## Claiming issues

Agents on several machines work this repo, all as the same GitHub user, so an issue is claimed before anyone works on it. A claim is the branch `claim/issue-<n>` on origin, holding one empty commit that names the agent and host. Git accepts only the first push of a new branch, so exactly one claimant wins. `scripts/issue.sh` does the whole cycle (bash; on Windows it runs under Git Bash):

```bash
scripts/issue.sh list                  # who holds what, on every machine, and for how long
scripts/issue.sh claim <n>             # claim and make the worktree ../Terrarium-<n>; exit 2: taken, pick another
scripts/issue.sh claim <n> --here      # claim for the worktree you're already in (a desktop-app worktree)
scripts/issue.sh renew <n>             # at the start of each session on long work
scripts/issue.sh land <n>              # verify, push the branch and open a PR to main; the merge closes the issue and that releases the claim
scripts/issue.sh release <n>           # giving up: push your branch and comment where it stands first
scripts/issue.sh reap [--dry-run]      # delete the claims whose issue is closed; the SessionStart hook runs it
```

A claim ends when its issue closes: `.github/workflows/release-claim.yml` reaps it within a minute of the merge (or of any close), whichever machine held it, so nobody runs `release` after a merge.

- **A hook enforces it.** `.claude/settings.json` runs `scripts/issue-guard.sh` before every `git commit` and `git push`. It refuses issue work (a branch named `issue-<n>`, or a commit saying `Closes #<n>`) unless this session holds the claim, from the worktree it was claimed for. A chore with no issue (a typo, a dependency bump) needs no claim: keep it off `issue-<n>` branches and out of `Closes` lines. A branch built on a sibling's carries the sibling's `Closes` line; once that issue is closed (the sibling merged), the push passes without its claim. A SessionStart hook reaps the claims on closed issues, then lists the rest.
- **Only the session that claimed an issue renews, releases or lands it,** and only from its worktree. An existing worktree for an issue you didn't claim is another session's: leave it alone. Never delete another agent's `claim/*` branch, with one exception: a claim on a closed issue guards nothing, and `scripts/issue.sh reap` is the one way to clear it.
- **Claims go stale after 24 hours.** `claim <n> --steal` takes over a stale one; check the issue's comments and `origin/issue-<n>` for work in progress first, and continue from it. Taking a younger claim is Chris's call only: `claim <n> --steal --override "<his reason>"`.
- Settings for this repo are in `scripts/issue.conf`. The tool is vendored from `skills/setup-claims` in chrischaps/Dashboard: change it there, not here. `bash scripts/issue-test.sh` tests it against a throwaway remote.
